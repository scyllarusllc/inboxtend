Gmail accounts

Checking…

    You can connect several Gmail accounts. Their mail is stored together and listed together (you can narrow the Inbox to one account), and each account is synced on its own. Add one with the button above and choose it in Google’s account picker.

    By default Inbox Tend can only read your mail. For each account you may additionally let it mark messages read or unread in Gmail. That needs Google’s broader gmail.modify permission, which Google describes as “read, compose and send emails … except permanent deletion”. Inbox Tend’s code only ever changes the unread flag of one message at a time (it cannot send, delete or trash mail), but the permission itself allows more, so grant it only on a server you trust. You can withdraw it here or at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

    Remove deletes that account’s sign-in from this server together with its saved messages, attachments, topics and translations; the other accounts and your tasks are kept. It does not revoke Google’s permission; do that at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

    Time and tasks

    Mail sync

    Changes apply to the next sync. Already stored mail is kept. The Gmail searches themselves (INBOX_TEND_SYNC_QUERY, INBOX_TEND_SYNC_SENT_QUERY) are set in .env.

    AI assistant

    Uses an AI command-line tool installed on this server to suggest tasks (with quoted evidence from the message) and to draft replies. Off by default. When on, pressing an AI button sends that one message’s sender, date, subject and text, and optionally small text attachments, to the provider’s service (OpenAI for Codex, Anthropic for Claude) through this server’s login for that tool. Nothing is sent automatically, no reply is ever sent, and every result is only a suggestion you review.

    The test sends a fixed message with no mail data, using the saved provider and model (save first). The program paths are set by the server operator with INBOX_TEND_CODEX_BIN and INBOX_TEND_CLAUDE_BIN in .env, not here.

    Translation

    Shows a translation of the open message in a third column on the Inbox page. It uses the provider and model chosen under AI assistant above. Off by default. When on, every message you open is sent to that provider (OpenAI for Codex, Anthropic for Claude) to be translated, without a button press. Each translation is saved in this server’s database, so a message is translated only once per language and reopening it costs nothing. Attachments are not translated.

    Sender details

    Shows a country flag next to the people you correspond with, and a small card on the open message and in the People list. Off by default.

    Guessed by the AI (marked as such, can be wrong): country and city, company and job title (only if their signature says so), the language they write in, what they are to you (client, colleague, friend, recruiter, vendor, newsletter, automated …) and their writing style (formal or casual, concise or long). It never guesses gender, age, ethnicity, religion, health or political views, and has no field for them.

    Counted from your own mail, no AI: the time where they are now (from the timezone of their messages, with a hint when it is night), since when you know them, how many emails, how fast they usually reply to you, and the hour they usually write.

    When on, each new sender is sent once to the provider chosen under AI assistant (OpenAI for Codex, Anthropic for Claude): display name, email address, the timezone offset their messages are dated with, up to two subject lines and short excerpts of up to two messages they sent you (the first and last 500 characters, where the greeting and the signature are; quoted replies and links removed; never attachments, and never mail you sent), 20 senders per request. Results are saved in this server’s database, so every sender is asked about only once. Details are only filled in when the text supports them; with no clue the field stays empty.

    Topic icons

    Puts a small icon on every message for what it is about, so you can see at a glance which mail is about money, work, love, friends and family, travel, shopping, meetings, account security, health, legal matters, education, tech, social networks, news, events, food or promotions. A message can have up to two. On the open message the topics are buttons: press one to see all mail about it (you can also search topic:money). Off by default.

    When on, each new message is sent once to the provider chosen under AI assistant (OpenAI for Codex, Anthropic for Claude): the other party’s name and address, the subject and the first 400 characters of the text (quoted replies and links removed; never attachments), 20 messages per request. This includes mail you sent. The answers are saved in this server’s database, so every message is asked about only once, and only the most recent 400 not yet labelled messages are handled per run. The topics are a guess by the AI and can be wrong; a message that fits none of the topics gets no icon.

    Gmail connection, mail sync, AI, translation, sender-detail and topic settings above are saved together.
